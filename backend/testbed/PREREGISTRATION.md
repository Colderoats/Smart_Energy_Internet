# Pre-registration: testbed + paper-reproduction settings

Written 2026-10-12, **before any measured model/twin/FL/contract result existed** (datasets were being generated but no model, twin or contract metric had been computed).
Every setting below is either taken from the paper text (cited as *J* = journal Draft 5, *C* = conference Revised Final) or has a stated physical / engineering / compute reason.
After results exist a setting may change only (a) to fix a bug, (b) because the paper text was misread, or (c) if it is a hyperparameter the paper itself says was tuned on validation data (mu, eta, E, LR, K, H, M, tau, epochs). Every such change is appended to the **Change log** at the bottom with its reason.
Fault magnitudes, noise levels, load profiles and block times are never changed to move a metric toward its paper number. Paper numbers are never read by any simulator, trainer or evaluator (they live only in `experiments/paper_targets.json`, read by the comparison step).

All outputs carry `provenance: "simulated_testbed"`.

## B. Journal topologies (J Table III) - node and line counts are structural and asserted by tests
Common: 11 kV (A, B) / 12.66 kV (C) pandapower networks; load power factor 0.95 lagging; each load bus has 20 % flexible share (curtail/shift); anti-islanding: a section without the grid connection is de-energised unless an action explicitly enables islanded operation. Node kinds: grid, solar, wind, hydro, load bus, collector; storage is a component of a bus; a *prosumer bus* is a bus with generation and load. **No households.**
* **A radial** (6 nodes, 5 lines; 2 solar + 1 wind): n0 grid slack; n1 collector; n2 load bus 350 kW + storage 400 kWh/150 kW; n3 solar prosumer (cap 400 kW, load 80 kW); n4 solar prosumer (300 kW, load 60 kW); n5 wind prosumer (350 kW, load 100 kW). Lines 0-1, 1-2, 1-3, 2-4, 2-5; 4 km, r/x = 0.5/0.3 ohm/km, rating 0.05 kA. Table III lists only 2 solar + 1 wind/hydro; the other three nodes are grid, collector and load bus.
* **B meshed** (14 nodes, 20 lines; 4 solar + 2 wind/hydro): graph = IEEE 14-bus branch list (`pandapower.networks.case14`, 14 buses / 20 branches), re-built as an 11 kV microgrid (the case's transformers/shunts are not used); grid n0; solar n2 (300 kW), n7 (300), n11 (250), n13 (250); wind n5 (400), hydro n9 (350); collectors n3, n8; load buses n1 (220 kW), n4 (260, +storage 500 kWh/200 kW), n6 (180), n10 (200, +storage 400/150), n12 (150); prosumer loads 50-90 kW on source buses. All 20 lines closed; every line has a breaker. 4 km, same cable data, 0.05 kA.
* **C IEEE 33-bus** (`pandapower.networks.case33bw()`, Baran & Wu): 33 buses, 32 lines + 5 normally-open tie lines = 37 lines; line number = paper number (line 12-13 = index 11, tie switch 33 = index 32). Loads = the case's aggregated bus loads. Added: 6 solar at buses 6, 13, 18, 25, 29, 33 and 3 wind/hydro (wind 11, hydro 22, wind 31), 450 kW each (paper bus numbers, 1-based); storage 600 kWh/250 kW at 8 and 24, 500/200 at 15 and 30; line rating 0.25 kA.
* Cable data (r/x 0.5/0.3 ohm/km at 4 km) chosen so the base case has voltages 0.95-1.05 pu at peak and the weak-feeder rerouting produces visible voltage drops (calibrated before any measured run: base-case probe only).

## C. Conference testbed (C Sec III-A, experiment config only)
12 nodes (4 solar, 4 wind, 4 hydro; node i has type `[solar, wind, hydro][i mod 3]`) in a ring with 4 chords (0-6, 1-7, 3-9, 4-10); 365 days (non-leap) at 15 min = 35,040 samples/node, 420,480 total; split 70/15/15 chronological (test = days 310-364, i.e. from 6 November). Nameplate 50 kW at every node (C1: chosen as a modest DER size; the paper gives none), V_nom 400 V, pf 0.95. Seasons: summer Mar-May, monsoon Jun-Sep, winter otherwise. Solar: clear-sky irradiance 1000 sin(elev)^1.2 with a seasonal gain, cloud cover AR(1) with seasonal means 0.20/0.60/0.25, Kasten-Czeplak attenuation, 0.85 derating. Wind: AR(1) speed with seasonal means 5.5/7.5/4.5 m/s, cubic power curve 3-12 m/s, rated to 25. Hydro: seasonal flow 0.35/1.0/0.5 with slow AR(1) noise. Faults (text): geometric inter-arrival with mean 8 days per node, duration U(1, 8) h, output factor U(0, 0.7), voltage dip U(3, 8) %, the live neighbour with the most spare capacity takes **50 %** of the lost power (PICKUP; paper says "part"). Twin expected state = healthy value x (1 + AR(1) error): power 5 % (text), voltage 1 % (C2: voltage is measured more accurately; the paper gives only "5 %"), correlation 0.5. Node counts as tripped (links drop from A(t)) below 2 % of nameplate. 7 features per step: power/cap, V/Vnom, I/Inom, resource driver (irradiance, wind speed/25, flow), sin hour, cos hour, sin day-of-year. Window 8, horizon 1.
* Twin (C Eqs 1-6): state = [P, V, I] per unit, Euclidean deviation; w = 4 samples (C3: 1 h; paper gives w only symbolically); beta, gamma by balanced logistic regression on the train partition per source type; tau on validation, sweep 0.30-0.90 step 0.05, maximise F1 s.t. FAR <= 1 % (text), tau shared by the 12 nodes of a dataset; Eq 5 lambda = 0.5 kW per ring-hop (C3), dist = ring index distance; fallbacks split -> storage (no storage at conference nodes, so empty) -> shed non-critical (30 % of dP) -> operator alert. Fault classification over 5 datasets (seeds 0-4); Table V by 5-fold blocked CV over the year; high load output > 60 %, low load 5-30 % of nameplate (text).
* Forecasting (C Table III): TA-GNN 8x7 -> 32 hidden, 2 TA layers K=2 ReLU, 2-layer MLP, Adam 3e-3, batch 256, MSE, 15 epochs keep best validation epoch; baselines LSTM, GRU (hidden 32), fixed-adjacency GCN with skip, TGNN with skip, TA-GNN on the fixed base adjacency; 3 seeds, dataset seed 0.
* FL (C Sec IV-D): 6 clients = (season x high/low-load-window) groups; IID = random equal split; Dirichlet alpha = 0.5 / 0.1 over the 6 groups' windows; 30 rounds, 2 local epochs (full pass over the client's windows), mu selected from {0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1} by validation RMSE of FedProx(fixed) at alpha = 0.5, seed 0; FedAvg and FedProx-fixed weight clients by n_k; adaptive weights Eqs 11-12 (rho_k from the client's validation loss); 3 seeds; rounds = first round whose validation RMSE is within 5 % of its best.

## D. Journal simulator (J Sec V; settings the paper lacks have stated reasons)
24 h at 5 s = 17,280 samples (asserted); 3 iterations per topology (seeds 1000(i+1) + {A:1,B:2,C:3}); self-healing evaluation uses 3 **separate held-out scenario sets** per topology generated by the same generator (seed offset 50,000) so no fault is seen in training; Table VIII uses a dedicated scenario set with 20 outages of the line between buses 12-13 at random instants (seed offset 90,000). Node features: generation kW, consumption kW (served), voltage pu, current kA (largest in-service incident line), SOC; measurement noise: power 1 %, voltage 0.2 %, current 0.5 %. Profiles: bus load = base/0.75 x daily shape (morning/evening peaks) x (1 + OU(tau 60 s, 3 %) + white 0.5 %); solar clear-sky bell x cloud OU(300 s); wind OU(600 s) around 7 m/s with the cubic curve; hydro 0.7 +- slow OU(3600 s, 5 %). Storage rule: charge on net surplus, discharge half of the deficit, SOC in [0.1, 0.9], 95 % efficiency, start 0.5. These profiles are synthetic (the repo has no ESP32 recording of the paper's nodes), the Open-Meteo / Kelmarsh data are **not** used, and they are labelled simulated.
Faults: 5 of each of 4 types per iteration (line outage; feeder short-circuit = a persistent high-impedance fault drawing 1.6-2.6x the line rating at the line's far end, pf 0.3, not tripped instantaneously so its signature lasts until switched out; sudden loss of generation = a generator output goes to 0; load surge = x U(1.8, 2.5) on one load bus); random instants with >= 20 min between starts and none in the first hour / last 33 min; duration (time to repair) U(5, 15) min. Numerical fallback: if a short-circuit power flow does not converge the shunt is re-tried at 0.6x then 0.35x (counted in the manifest). Labels come from the injection log only; `c(t)` = class of the fault active at t.
Demand target `Y(t+1)` = requested (not served) bus demand at t+1; forecast metrics are over load-carrying non-grid buses.

## E. Journal twin evaluation (J Sec IV-B, V-B)
* **Cost** (Eq 7): J = 0.6 E_uns_norm + 0.3 V_viol + 0.1 N_sw/4 (w1 largest, as the paper requires). E_uns = unserved power x 600 s planning horizon, normalised by demand energy over the horizon; V_viol = (sum over energised buses of excursions beyond [0.95, 1.05] pu + sum of line overload fractions)/N; N_sw = breaker operations + 1 if islanding + 1 if curtailing. Hard feasibility: 0.90 <= V <= 1.10 pu, line loading <= 120 %, power flow converged. If no candidate is feasible the least-J one is used and flagged.
* **Omega**: open/isolate the faulted line; close any normally-open tie; island a section (enable grid-forming storage/generation, optionally after opening one further switch); curtail the flexible 20 % of affected loads; full storage discharge (generation loss and surge). Candidates are the combinations; each is simulated with pandapower on a copy of the believed state.
* **Default scheme**: threshold protection (alarm when a line exceeds 1.2x rating, a bus drops below 0.9 pu or is lost, or a generator drops > 50 % in 12 samples; persisted 3 samples) + fixed rule sequence (isolate; then close tie switches in list order, keep the first feasible one that reduces unserved load; no islanding, no curtailment, no cost evaluation). **Twin**: alarm when the TA-GNN fault head predicts a non-normal class for 2 consecutive samples; location from measurements only (breaker status, line current, bus power); decision latency 1 sample.
* Timing: one switching operation 2 s (remote switch), verification 1 sample (5 s) after operations, backup protection (feeder trips after 3 samples above 125 % loading or on a persistent short-circuit) in both schemes. T_rec = t_restored - t_fault for faults that interrupted supply (t_restored = when unserved power reaches its final value); E_uns = integral of unserved power from t_fault to the repair instant. Detection F1 (Table VII) = sample-level F1 of the alarm signal vs the injection-log label over the scenario; it is **not** the Table X fault-head F1 (conflict 8).
* Self-optimisation (no fault): reschedule storage against the forecast; reported as grid-import reduction in the Topologies KPI panel, not a paper table.

## F. Journal models
L = 12 (60 s window); features min-max normalised over the train part (Eq 9, per feature); Eq 10 A_hat from the switch states of every snapshot; TA layer = Eq 11 in dense form (checked equal to PyG TAGConv in tests); heads Eqs 12-13 (mean-pooled embedding -> 5-class softmax); loss Eq 14 with lambda_F = lambda_C = 1, plain cross-entropy; Adam; **batch = N snapshots** (N = 6/14/33; the paper says "batch size equals the number of nodes"); 80/20 chronological split; Table IV options as the paper (25 epochs); Table V: 3_LRS and 3_NLRS 250 epochs, 2_NLRS 150, scheduler 0.001 -> 0.0001 at epoch 50 -> 0.00001 at epoch 150; GCN baseline = first-order propagation, same 4 options for 25 epochs (equal tuning budget), then its best option 250 epochs with the scheduler. Validation F1 = macro-F1 over the classes present. Seed = iteration index. Dropout: none (not specified). The search is run on every iteration; the options carried to Table V are the paper's (3 and 2) and the data's ranking is recorded next to them.
Journal FL (J Sec 4.5 / 5.1.1): one client per node; client k trains the Table-V model on its own node's demand (Eq 14 with the MSE over node k only) and the fault CE; n_k = local training samples; v_k = Eq 14 on client k's held-out validation windows; Eq 16 weights; `local epoch` = pass over a random 10 % of the training windows (batch = N snapshots) (compute reason: a full pass for 33 clients x E x 100+ rounds is infeasible on this machine); at most 150 rounds; grid mu {0.001, 0.01, 0.1}, eta {0,1,5,10}, E {1,3,5} on iteration 0 selected by (rounds to target, then final validation loss); selected configs rerun on iterations 0-2; FedAvg tuned over E, FedProx over mu x E. Global validation loss = Eq 14 on the pooled validation set. **Target** = (centralised validation loss of the same iteration) + 0.0012 (placeholder resolved as the bracketed text says; the absolute 0.0130 is also reported). C_comm = 2 R K |w| beta with the real parameter count, beta = 4.

## G. Trading and contract
EnergyMarket on a local Hardhat node (Solidity 0.8.24). Prices (milli-currency/kWh): seller ask U(60, 110), buyer maximum U(120, 140), grid import 150, feed-in 40; trading interval 5 min; offers capped by the forecast surplus (forecast demand from the TA-GNN, persistence generation), metered quantity = realised surplus/deficit; matching sorted by price (cheapest ask to highest bid), conference Eq 14-15 rule available as an option; feasibility by pandapower on the active topology, infeasible quantity bisected down. Gas per operation from receipts; latency measured with automine and with **1 s interval mining** (chosen for realism before running: permissioned/PoA chains typically use 1-5 s blocks). Table VIII (conference): 5/10/20/50 registered nodes, pairs post an offer and a bid and settle, 5 rounds x 3 repeats, sequential submission.

## Change log
**Amendment 1 - 2026-10-12, made BEFORE any journal model, journal-twin, FL or trading metric existed** (the only measured results at that time were the conference twin runs, which this amendment does not touch).
Reason: a CPU micro-benchmark (not a metric) showed the literal settings are infeasible on this machine: 6.4 ms of CPU per optimisation step, so batch = N snapshots would be 2,304 steps/epoch for topology A and ~13 CPU-hours for Tables IV-V alone, and the FL grid (36 configs x 3 topologies, 33 clients) would take months.
Amended settings (compute-driven; none of them touches a fault magnitude, noise level, load profile or block time):
1. Journal training batch = **32 snapshots** for all topologies (= N for topology C; the paper's "batch size equals the number of nodes" is kept for C and relaxed for A (6) and B (14)). Epoch counts, learning rates, scheduler, options and splits are exactly the paper's.
2. Journal FL: a local epoch passes over a random **5 %** of the training windows (was 10 %), batch 32 snapshots; at most **100 rounds** (was 150). The full mu x eta x E grid is run on topologies A and B (iteration 0); topology C evaluates the two configurations selected on A and B for each family (a 2-config validation grid) because 33 clients x 36 configs is infeasible. FedAvg/FedProx grids as before.
3. The conference twin still uses the pre-registered balanced logistic fit for beta/gamma. Its validation sweep ended at the edge (tau = 0.9, the FAR <= 1 % constraint was not met at any tau); this is reported, and no setting was changed after seeing it.

**Amendment 2 - 2026-10-12, before any journal twin metric was computed** (a smoke test on a 2,600-step toy dataset exposed that the first T_rec implementation scored a scheme that never restores anything as T_rec = 0; this is a bug fix of the definition, not a tuning step).
"All the restorable load has been re-energised" (journal Eq. 22) is implemented as: the *restorable floor* U* is the least unserved power that any feasible, non-curtailing action of Omega leaves on the true faulted state (an evaluation-only oracle; neither scheme sees it). A fault counts for T_rec only if a scheme leaves more than U* unserved at some time; t_restored is the end of the last interval with unserved power above U*; a scheme that never reaches U* before repair gets T_rec = the repair instant (fault duration). E_uns still integrates all unserved power from t_fault to the repair instant.

**Amendment 3 - 2026-10-12, before any federated-learning metric existed** (compute: topology C needs ~40 ms of CPU per optimisation step, so one 33-client federation of 100 rounds costs ~2 CPU-hours).
Journal FL protocol: the full mu x eta x E grid (and the FedAvg-over-E / FedProx-over-mu-x-E grids) is run on **topology A, iteration 0**, selected on validation only (fewest rounds to the target, then lowest best validation loss). Topologies B and C use topology A's selected configuration of each family (no per-topology tuning). Topologies A and B are then run on iterations 0-2; topology C on iteration 0 only (more if time allows; the number of iterations behind each Table X cell is reported as n). This supersedes the grid description in amendment 1, item 2.

**Amendment 4 - 2026-10-12, at the user's request to finish training in about an hour; before any journal model metric was reported.** All journal epoch counts are scaled by 0.4 (search 25 -> 10; final 250 -> 100, 150 -> 60; scheduler breakpoints 50/150 -> 20/60, learning-rate values unchanged), and topology C trains on iteration 0 only (its twin evaluation and Table VI/VII/X cells therefore have n = 1). Topologies A and B keep 3 iterations. This is a compute reduction, not tuned on results; the papers' Table IV/V epoch counts are therefore not matched literally.

**Amendment 5 - 2026-10-12, bug fix after inspecting the first twin results (disclosed: those results were seen).** The short-circuit localiser picked the line with the largest current/rating ratio, which is the feeder head (it carries the fault current too), so the twin opened the wrong line and cut supply (topology C: up to 700 kWh unserved in one event). Fix: the faulted line is the overloaded line farthest from the grid in the in-service graph (fault current only flows source -> fault). No threshold, weight or timing was changed. The twin evaluations were re-run with the fixed localiser; the earlier numbers are discarded, not tuned between.

**Disclosure 6 - 2026-10-13, no setting changed (metric definition limits, found after the FL grid ran).** The journal Table X target is "centralised validation loss + 0.0012". On this testbed the centralised 3_LRS model overfits its fault head (validation loss 2.59 / 3.89 / 0.74 on A / B / C; see Table V), so every federated run satisfies that target at round 1 and "rounds to target" is 1 for all methods (0 % communication reduction, which is what the formula gives). The absolute 0.0130 target is never reached (best validation loss about 0.3). Both are reported as measured. The secondary "rounds to a common target" (first round with validation loss <= 1.05 x the best any method reached in that iteration) is reported next to them, labelled as a secondary, post-hoc comparison; the selection of mu / eta / E was made with the pre-registered rule (fewest rounds to target, then lowest best validation loss) and was not changed.
Interval-mining settings (Table XI latency realism, chosen before the runs): 1 s block interval; first 12 trading intervals of the held-out day (journal trading), 4 repetitions per operation in the contract benchmark.
Concurrency note: the trading jobs ran in parallel on separate local Hardhat nodes (SEI_RPC), and the FL farm ran at the same time, so wall-clock latencies in the trading runs are inflated and noisy; gas is unaffected.
